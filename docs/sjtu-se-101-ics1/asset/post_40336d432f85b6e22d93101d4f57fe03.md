## FIFA World Cup History

The FIFA World Cup is an international association football competition contested by the senior men's national teams of the members of the Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA), the sport's global governing body. The championship has been awarded every four years since the inaugural tournament in 1930, except in 1942 and 1946 when it was not held due to the Second World War.

The tournament consists of a qualification phase, held over the preceding three years, to determine which teams qualify for the tournament phase. In the tournament phase, 32 teams compete for the title at venues within the host nation over a period of about a month.

### History

The first World Cup was held in Uruguay in 1930. The host nation won the final against Argentina 4–2. The early years of the competition saw European and South American teams dominate the tournament. The trophy was designed by French artist Abel Lafleur and is made of gold plated sterling silver.

- 1930: Uruguay — Winner: Uruguay
- 1934: Italy — Winner: Italy
- 1938: France — Winner: Italy
- 1950: Brazil — Winner: Uruguay
- 1954: Switzerland — Winner: West Germany
- 1958: Sweden — Winner: Brazil
- 1962: Chile — Winner: Brazil
