## Properties of relations on a set

relation R on the set A

- Reflexive
  - ∀ a ((a,a) ∈ R), a ∈ A
- Irreflexive
  - ∀ a ((a,a) ∉ R), a ∈ A
- Symmetric
  - ∀ a ∀ b ((a,b) ∈ R → (b,a) ∈ R)  a,b ∈ A
- Antisymmetric
  - ∀ a ∀ b ((a,b) ∈ R ∧ (b,a) ∈ R → (a=b))  a,b ∈ A

## Properties of relations on a set

relation R on the set A

- Transitive
  - ∀a∀b∀c ((a,b)∈R ∧ (b,c)∈R → (a,c)∈R)
    a,b,c∈A

## Combining relations

- R1 and R2 are relations from A to B
  - R1 ∪ R2
  - R1 ∩ R2
  - R1-R2
  - R2-R1

## Combining relations

- R is a relation from A to B, S is a relation from B to C, then
  - The composite of R and S is the relation from A to C denoted as S∘R, for each (a,c) ∈ S∘R, there exits an element b ∈ B such as (a,b) ∈ R, (b,c) ∈ S

## N-ary relations

- **Definition**
  - Let A1, A2, … An be sets, an n-ary relation on these sets is a subset of A1×A2×…×An, A1, A2, … An are called domains of the relation, n is called its degree
- **Examples**
  - (D, 305, shanghai, beijing, 9:00pm)
  - (Zhangsan, G10101, english, A, 2009, Fall)

## Operations on N-ary relations

- Selection operator sC
  - Maps the n-ary relation R to the n-ary relation of all n-tuples from R that satisfy the condition C
- Projection operator P_i1,i2,...,im
  - Maps the n-tuple(a1,a2,...an) to the m-tuple(a_i1,a_i2,...,a_im), where m≤n

## Operations on N-ary relations

### Join operator $J_p(R,S)$

Let $R$ be a relation of degree $m$, $S$ a relation of degree $n$, $p\le m,p\le n$. $J_p(R,S)$ is a relation of degree $m+n-p$ that consists of all $(m+n-p)$-tuples $(a_1,a_2,\ldots a_{m-p},c_1,c_2,\ldots c_p,b_1,b_2,\ldots b_{n-p})$, where $(a_1,a_2,\ldots a_{m-p},c_1,c_2,\ldots c_p)$ belongs to $R$ and $(c_1,c_2,\ldots c_p,b_1,b_2,\ldots b_{n-p})$ belongs to $S$

## Representing Relations

- Represent a relation between finite sets
  - List its ordered pairs
  - Use 0-1 matrices (used in computer programs)
  - Directed graphs (for people to understand the relations better)

## Using matrices

- Suppose that R is a relation from A={a₁,a₂,…,aₘ} to B={b₁,b₂,…,bₙ}.
  - the elements have been listed in an arbitrary order (if A=B, then the order is the same).
  - use matrix M_R=[mᵢⱼ] to represent R

$$M_{ij}=\begin{cases}1\ldots\text{if }(a_i,b_j)\in R\\0\ldots\text{if }(a_i,b_j)\notin R\end{cases}$$
