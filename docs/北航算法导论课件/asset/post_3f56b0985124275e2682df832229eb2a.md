### RSA public-key cryptosystem

![](img/09c68ab421cfce4995ddad1430ef2637_583_0.png)

1. Select at random two large prime numbers *p* and *q* such that *p* ≠ *q*. The primes *p* and *q* might be, say, 512 bits each, 1024 bits, or more.
2. Compute *n* by the equation *n = p q*.
3. Let *φ(n) = (p - 1)(q - 1)*.
4. Select a small odd integer *e* that is relatively prime to *φ(n)*, i.e., gcd(*e*, *φ(n)*) = 1.
5. Compute *d* as the multiplicative inverse of *e*, modulo *φ(n)*, i.e., *e d ≡ 1 (mod φ(n))*.
6. Publish the pair *P = (e, n)* as his *RSA public key*.
7. Keep secret the pair *S = (d, n)* as his *RSA secret key*.

### Why is RSA encryption effective

![](img/09c68ab421cfce4995ddad1430ef2637_584_0.png)

![](img/09c68ab421cfce4995ddad1430ef2637_585_0.png)

### RSA is correct

Theorem 31.36:

S(P(M)) = S(M^e (mod n)) = (M^e (mod n))^d (mod n) = M^{ed} (mod n)
= M^{1+k φ(n)} (mod n) = M M^{k φ(n)} (mod n) = M (M^{(p-1)})^{k(q-1)} (mod n) ... = M (mod n)

(by Theorem 31.31, Fermat’s Theorem, if p is prime, M^{(p-1)} ≡ 1 (mod p))

### RSA is secure

S(P(M)) = S(M^e (mod n)) = M^{ed} (mod n) = ... = M

- If factoring n is easy, breaking the RSA cryptosystem is easy.
- Conversely, that if factoring large integers is hard, then breaking RSA is hard, is unproven.
- After two decades of research, however, no easier method has been found to break the RSA public-key cryptosystem than to factor n.
- In fact, the factoring of large integers is surprisingly difficult.
