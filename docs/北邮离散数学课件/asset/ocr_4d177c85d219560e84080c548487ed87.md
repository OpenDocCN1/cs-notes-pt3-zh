# A `semantic' proof
(to be made precise later)

∀x H(x) → M(x) (“Humans are mortal”) and ∀x G(x) → ¬M(x) (“Gods are immortal”). Suppose ∃x (H(x) ∧ G(x)). For example, H(a) ∧ G(a). Then By the first premisse, we have M(x). By the second premisse, we have ¬M(x). Contradiction! Therefore, it follows that ¬∃x (H(x) ∧ G(x)) (“No human is a god.”)
