
## Proof

If \(x\) and \(y\) are elements in \(B^n\), then
\[
f_H(x \oplus y) = (x \oplus y) * \mathbf{H}
= (x * \mathbf{H}) \oplus (y * \mathbf{H}) \quad \text{by Theorem 4}
= f_H(x) \oplus f_H(y).
\]
Hence \(f_H\) is a homomorphism from \(B^n\) to \(B^r\).

Q.E.D.

## Corollary 1

Let \(m\), \(n\), \(r\), \(\mathbf{H}\), and \(f_H\) be as in Theorem 5. Then
\[
N = \{x \in B^n \mid x * \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0}\}
\]
is a normal subgroup of \(B^n\).

### Proof

\(N\) is the kernel of the homomorphism \(f_H\), so it is a normal subgroup of \(B^n\).

## Encoding function

Define an encoding function \(e_H: B^m \to B^n\).

If \(b = b_1 b_2 \dots b_m\), let \(x = e_H(b) = b_1 b_2 \dots b_m x_1 x_2 \dots x_r\), where
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= b_1 \cdot h_{11} + b_2 \cdot h_{21} + \dots + b_m \cdot h_{m1}\\
x_2 &= b_1 \cdot h_{12} + b_2 \cdot h_{22} + \dots + b_m \cdot h_{m2}\\
&\vdots\\
x_r &= b_1 \cdot h_{1r} + b_2 \cdot h_{2r} + \dots + b_m \cdot h_{mr}
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

## Theorem 6

Let \(x = y_1 y_2 \dots y_m x_1 \dots x_r \in B^n\). Then
\[
x * \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0}
\]
if and only if
\[
x = e_H(b)
\]
for some \(b \in B^m\).

### Proof

Suppose that \(x * \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0}\). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
y_1 \cdot h_{11} + y_2 \cdot h_{21} + \cdots + y_m \cdot h_{m1} + x_1 &= 0\\
y_1 \cdot h_{12} + y_2 \cdot h_{22} + \cdots + y_m \cdot h_{m2} + x_2 &= 0\\
&\vdots\\
y_1 \cdot h_{1r} + y_2 \cdot h_{2r} + \cdots + y_m \cdot h_{mr} + x_r &= 0
\end{aligned}
\]

Note that \(x_i + x_i = 0\). So add \(x_i\) to the \(i\)-th equation and get
\[
x_i = y_1 \cdot h_{1i} + y_2 \cdot h_{2i} + \cdots + y_m \cdot h_{mi}.
\]

Letting \(b_1 = y_1\), \(b_2 = y_2, \dots, b_m = y_m\), we see that \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_r\) satisfy the equations in (1). Thus \(b = b_1 b_2 \dots b_m \in B^m\) and \(x = e_H(b)\).

Conversely, if \(x = e_H(b)\), the equations in (1) can be rewritten by adding \(x_i\) to both sides of the \(i\)-th equation, \(i = 1, 2, \dots, r\), as
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_1 \cdot h_{11} + b_2 \cdot h_{21} + \cdots + b_m \cdot h_{m1} + x_1 &= 0\\
b_1 \cdot h_{12} + b_2 \cdot h_{22} + \cdots + b_m \cdot h_{m2} + x_2 &= 0\\
&\vdots\\
b_1 \cdot h_{1r} + b_2 \cdot h_{2r} + \cdots + b_m \cdot h_{mr} + x_r &= 0
\end{aligned}
\]
which shows \(x * \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{0}\).

Q.E.D.
