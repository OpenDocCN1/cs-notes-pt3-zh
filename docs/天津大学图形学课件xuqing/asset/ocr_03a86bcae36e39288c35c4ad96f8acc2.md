# How to Calculate \((\vec{R} \cdot \vec{V})\)

+   - \((\vec{R} \cdot \vec{V})\) can be approximated by \((\vec{H} \cdot \vec{N})\)
+   - Suppose the light source and the viewer are both at infinity, \(\vec{H}\) is constant

\(\vec{H} = \frac{1}{2}(\vec{L} + \vec{V})\), normalized as \(\frac{\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}+\vec{V})}{\left|\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}+\vec{V})\right|}\)

![](bbox=[0.313, 0.451, 0.651, 0.743])

+   - \(\vec{H}\) can be thought as a normal of a virtual specular surface
+   - A compensation can be obtained by adjusting \(n\) *(specular reflection exponent)*
