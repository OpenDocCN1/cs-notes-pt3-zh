## Screen Coordinates

- The <u>normalized window</u> will be mapped to a specified spot on the screen, called the Viewport.
- Screen coordinates are measured in pixels and are hardware specific.
- Several viewports can be defined on the screen.
- Viewports can overlap.

## From Window to Viewport

![](img/258e4144ed4b66c8fd4b0f298118df23_187_0.png)

$$
\begin{cases}
x_v = A x_w + B\\
y_v = C y_w + D
\end{cases}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{VXR - VXL}{WXR - WXL}, & B &= VXL - A \cdot WXL\\
C &= \frac{VYT - VYB}{WYT - WYB}, & D &= VYB - C \cdot WYB
\end{aligned}
$$

## Local Illumination

A lighting model is used to determine the shade of a point on a surface using the position, orientation and characteristics of the surface and the light source(s) illuminating it. A typical lighting model consists of three separate components - **ambient, diffuse and specular**.

## Local Illumination

### Diffuse Reflection Model

![](img/258e4144ed4b66c8fd4b0f298118df23_191_0.png)

$$
I_{dr}=I_{Lr}\cdot K_{dr}(\vec{L}\cdot\vec{N})
$$
$$
I_{dg}=I_{Lg}\cdot K_{dg}(\vec{L}\cdot\vec{N})
$$
$$
I_{db}=I_{Lb}\cdot K_{db}(\vec{L}\cdot\vec{N})
$$

$$
I_d=K_d\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left[I_{L_i}\cdot(\vec{L_i}\cdot\vec{N})\right]
$$

$$
I_d=I_L\cdot K_d\cos\theta
$$

- Know as *Lambertian reflection*
- It is view-independent
- Reflection coefficient depends on the characteristics of material and input light wavelength
- for multiple light sources

## How to Calculate \((\vec{R} \cdot \vec{V})\)

- \((\vec{R} \cdot \vec{V})\) can be approximated by \((\vec{H} \cdot \vec{N})\)
- Suppose the light source and the viewer are both at infinity, \(\vec{H}\) is constant

\(\vec{H} = \frac{1}{2}(\vec{L} + \vec{V})\), normalized as \(\frac{\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}+\vec{V})}{\left|\frac{1}{2}(\vec{L}+\vec{V})\right|}\)

![](img/258e4144ed4b66c8fd4b0f298118df23_194_0.png)

- \(\vec{H}\) can be thought as a normal of a virtual specular surface
- A compensation can be obtained by adjusting \(n\) *(specular reflection exponent)*
